# SocialSeal Carousel Production

## Overview

Produce a carousel as a real visual artifact, not a slide-outline document. Use
SocialSeal to choose the audience job and reference mechanisms; use approved brand
facts and assets to make the content credible; use prototypes and phone-size QA to
make the design publishable.

Read `references/creative-production-gates.md`, `references/content-lenses.md`, and
`references/evidence-and-confidence.md`.

## Inputs

Required:

- approved platform, market, language, query/topic, and audience job
- opportunity evidence and selected benchmark direction
- brand utility bank, voice/constraints, and approved CTA
- target slide count/aspect ratio and requested delivery state

Good to have:

- brand fonts, colors, logos, prior posts, and design tokens
- owned image/video bank with rights and location/subject metadata
- production tool/environment and export requirements

If these are missing, route to `socialseal-strategy-readiness`,
`socialseal-opportunity-analysis`, `socialseal-competitor-content-analysis`, or
`socialseal-asset-planning` before designing.

## Workflow

1. **Confirm the delivery contract.** State whether the user expects an outline,
   prototype, editable source, exported JPG/PNG slides, or a post-ready package.
2. **Confirm evidence and utility.** Restate the viewer question, selected query,
   real brand answer/tips, exclusions, and facts needing confirmation. Do not fill
   slides with generic category copy because an itinerary or insider fact is absent.
3. **Analyze references.** For representative surfacing carousels/posts, capture the
   cover mechanism, hierarchy, text density, slide sequence, image treatment, proof
   objects, vocabulary, CTA, and why the mechanism fits the query. Label
   metadata-only references.
4. **Offer visual routes.** Present 2-3 coherent directions grounded in the brand and
   references. Include fonts, colors, layout logic, image treatment, writing style,
   and trade-offs. Do not offer cosmetic variants that leave the core decision
   unchanged.
5. **Build the slide architecture.** Give each slide one job: hook, orientation,
   compare, step, proof, practical detail, decision aid, or CTA. Prefer concrete
   information such as routes, times, maps, prices, itineraries, mistakes, signs, or
   local proof over vague destination adjectives.
6. **Run the asset gate.** Map every slide to a reviewed asset. Mark rights and
   location/subject confidence (`verified`, `likely`, `unknown`). Present owned,
   rights-cleared stock, reshoot, generated-reference, or concept-change choices for
   gaps. Never imply an unknown stock image is a real place.
7. **Prototype first.** Produce the cover and one representative body slide using
   the actual visual system and assets. Ask the user to select/adjust the direction
   before rendering the full set.
8. **Produce all slides.** Use a capable design/rendering environment and retain an
   editable source when possible. If the environment can only write copy or a design
   brief, say so; do not claim that files were built.
9. **Render and verify.** Inspect every exported slide at phone size for hierarchy,
   crop, contrast, safe areas, text size, consistency, sequence, factual accuracy,
   branding, rights, and file integrity.
10. **Assign delivery state.** Use prototype, draft, production candidate, or
    post-ready exactly as defined in the creative-production reference. List blockers
    beside the state.

## Design Rules

- Derive styling from approved brand tokens and selected social references.
- Avoid default gradients, floating "option"/"step" pills, arbitrary rounded cards,
  excessive empty space, generic AI iconography, and stock-marketing language unless
  the reference/brand direction explicitly supports them.
- Keep first-frame copy legible and compelling without clickbait detached from the
  content payoff.
- Use creator/local vocabulary and useful detail depth from real examples without
  copying their language or design.
- Do not let text obscure the subject or place essential copy in platform UI zones.
- Do not call a visually complete slide truthful when its image, claim, or location
  remains unverified.

## Output

- evidence-backed slide outline and approved visual route
- asset map with rights and confidence
- approved cover/body prototype
- editable source when supported
- exported slide files with dimensions
- caption/CTA direction and evidence note
- QA checklist, delivery state, and unresolved blockers

## Done Means

Post-ready requires all exported slides to pass factual, location, rights, brand,
visual, mobile-readability, sequence, and file-integrity checks. A Markdown outline,
design brief, or set of placeholders is not a finished carousel.

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] Query, audience job, and brand utility are approved.
- [ ] Visual direction is anchored in viewed references and brand tokens.
- [ ] Every slide has one job and a reviewed asset.
- [ ] Rights and location/subject confidence are recorded.
- [ ] Cover and body prototype were approved before full production.
- [ ] Exported files were visually inspected at phone size.
- [ ] Delivery state is accurate and blockers are visible.
